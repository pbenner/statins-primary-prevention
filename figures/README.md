# Figures for investigating statins in primary prevention

Start with **1, 2 and 3**. They address benefit before established disease, the size of that benefit, and the mortality controversy. The numbered items below refer to original published figures; PDF pages and their renders preserve the printed content. Run `scripts/collect_figures.py` after retrieving the sources to rebuild these original-figure downloads, page renders, and `figures/manifest.json`. For extracted numerical data and clearer redrawn plots, see the [reproducible analysis](../analysis/README.md) and its [overview figure](statins_overview.pdf).

## 1 Previous vascular disease and relative benefit

**CTT 2012 Figure 2** separates people with and without previous vascular disease. Both summary diamonds favor treatment: rate ratios 0.75 and 0.80 per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction. The between-group heterogeneity test is P=0.10. This directly tests the claim and shows no Simpson reversal. Individual subgroup bars use **99% intervals**; diamonds use 95%. The outcome is major vascular events, not total deaths. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3437972/#fig2)

## 2 Absolute benefit at different baseline risks

**CTT 2012 Figure 5** shows predicted events prevented per 1,000 over five years, by baseline risk and LDL reduction. These are **modeled projections**, not raw trial counts. Read the numbers on the bars rather than their perspective. Benefit increases with baseline risk and LDL reduction. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3437972/#fig5)

Local download: `02_CTT_2012_figure_5.jpg` (regenerated by `scripts/collect_figures.py`).

## 3 Mortality across individual primary prevention trials

**USPSTF 2022 Figure 3**, PDF page 10 (`03_USPSTF_2022_figure_3_page.pdf`, generated locally), shows why an uncertain result in an individual trial need not imply an uncertain pooled result. In the top panel, many intervals cross 1, while the pooled mortality estimate is 0.92 (0.87–0.98). The middle panel's cardiovascular mortality estimate remains uncertain. Rows marked with a superscript identify extracted primary prevention populations. [Original review](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.12138)

Local download: `03_USPSTF_2022_figure_3.gif` (regenerated by `scripts/collect_figures.py`).

## 4 Different endpoints give different answers

**USPSTF 2022 Figure 4**, PDF page 15 (`04_USPSTF_2022_figure_4_page.pdf`, generated locally), puts mortality, MI, stroke and harms together. Compare relative effects with absolute event proportions. Outcomes overlap and have different trial sets and follow-up; do not add them or treat crude pooled proportions as adjusted treatment effects. Its diabetes summary predates the larger 2024 analysis below. [Original review](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.12138)

Local download: `04_USPSTF_2022_figure_4.gif` (regenerated by `scripts/collect_figures.py`).

## 5 An early trial and the course of events over time

**WOSCOPS Figures 2 and 3**, PDF page 4 (`05_WOSCOPS_1995_page_4.pdf`, generated locally), show cumulative events with pravastatin and placebo. Figure 2 concerns nonfatal MI or coronary death. In Figure 3, compare nonfatal MI with all deaths: P<0.001 versus P=0.051. Check each panel's vertical scale and the declining numbers at risk before interpreting separation. The trial excluded previous MI but permitted some other vascular disease. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199511163332001)

Local download: `05_WOSCOPS_1995_page_4.png` (regenerated by `scripts/collect_figures.py`).

## 6 Older adults and the remaining uncertainty

**CTT 2019 Figure 4** separates age groups by previous vascular disease. The wide interval for primary prevention above age 75 illustrates the earlier uncertainty; crossing 1 does not establish no benefit. This figure predates STAREE's 2026 results, which are discussed in the [overview](../OVERVIEW.md). [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6429627/#fig4)

## 7 Muscle symptoms attributable to treatment

**CTT 2022 Figure 3** compares symptom rates with placebo, separating the first year from later years. The treatment-related excess is the difference between groups, not the entire statin bar. The right-hand column gives that absolute excess per 1,000 person-years. These trials include primary and secondary prevention. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7613583/#fig3)

## 8 Diabetes risk and baseline glycaemia

**CTT 2024 Figure 2** distinguishes background diabetes incidence from the estimated excess with statins. Red segments assume a constant relative treatment effect within each intensity category. Absolute excess depends on baseline risk. The high and lower intensity panels involve different trials and ascertainment, so they are not a direct randomized comparison between doses. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615958/#F2)

Local download: `08_CTT_2024_diabetes_figure_2.jpg` (regenerated by `scripts/collect_figures.py`).

## Reading the forest plots

A ratio of **1** means no difference. For an undesirable outcome, values below 1 favor treatment. Horizontal lines show uncertainty; larger squares generally indicate greater statistical weight. Diamonds summarize trials or subgroups. An interval crossing 1 is not proof of no effect. A significant result in one subgroup and a nonsignificant result in another do not themselves demonstrate an interaction.
